## 1. Executive summary

The code state is the first one after the September refactorings that split the appender into `RecordPlan` and `KafkaTransport`, merged the two dispatchers into `BoundedWorkerDispatcher`, moved the self-logging decisions behind `SelfLoggingGuard` and removed the Spring Boot parent - 75 commits and roughly 1 600 changed production lines since the last defect analysis. The production side came out clean at the levels that matter: **no Critical and no High defect in production code.** The hot path is lock-free as CONTRIBUTING demands, the hand-off/close protocol of the shared dispatcher skeleton is sound (in-flight ownership, check-then-act reclaim, drain-before-interrupt all hold on re-derivation), the transport's rollback covers the realistic failure, and every fix of the previous chain that still has a home in the refactored code was verified in place. The one production defect worth a remediation cycle is an operability contract broken in both directions: every configuration error this library raises itself - unknown topic class, marker mapped twice, blank identity, idempotence-incompatible tuning - is reported to the operator **without its message** unless `<debug>` is on, because the plan stage and the properties builder were routed under the credential-withholding path meant for the Kafka client's construction failure; README, guide and CHANGELOG all promise a "named error" (finding 2). The remaining production findings are Low: two gaps in the "open as a transaction" contract, two lifecycle corners outside ADR-0004's stated one-life model (detach after start, stop before start), an inventory asymmetry in the fallback queue gauges, an undetected not-started fallback appender, and binding failures reported at INFO.

The weight of this pass therefore lies on the tests, where the refactorings left three false-confidence spots. The most serious is at the composition root: the test that claims to pin same-thread reentry protection (the `StackOverflowError` guard) has, since the asynchronous dispatch, been exercising the worker-thread-name path instead, so the appender's `enter()`/`exit()` bracket - the only protection against an encoder or metrics hook that logs on the caller thread - is tested nowhere (finding 1, High). The blank-default-topic test tolerates the withheld message and thereby masks finding 2 (finding 3); the Spring binding's idempotency test and the metrics "appender tag on every meter" test cannot detect the regressions they describe (findings 4, 5). Everything else is Low and mostly coverage: the interrupt phase of the two-phase close, the `claimDiversion` stand-down at unit level, the close-timeout propagation to the producers, the cap boundaries, the deferral branches of the binding - and the fuzz targets' documented regression mode runs on the empty input only because no seed corpus was ever committed.

**Test verdict.** *Reliability as a safety net:* high for what the suite sets out to prove - accounting, lifecycle and exactly-once are pinned with latches, exact counts and mutation-resistant assertions, and the previous chain's P1 (leaks) and P4 (rationale blocks: 300 of 300 now) are verifiably closed. The three false-confidence spots named above are the exceptions, and they sit at score-5 (reentry) and score-3 (bindings) locations. Testability is good where the structure allows: clocks are injected in the throttle, workers are anchored with latches; the shutdown budgets and the send-duration timer still burn real time (P3, deferred by decision). *Usage and pyramid:* unit-heavy and fast - ~300 test methods, all in the default run except one Testcontainers test and one external-contract characterization; the composition root is carried by one 65-test class, which is structurally justified. Fuzzing contributes no evidential value to the PR build (empty corpus). *Most significant gaps and anomalies:*
- the appender-level reentry bracket (`KafkaAppender.kt:451/527`) has no test that would go red if it were removed (finding 1);
- the operator-visible text of start-up refusals is asserted nowhere with `debug=false`, and the one test that could is disjunctive (finding 3);
- binding idempotency and the multi-appender tag guarantee are asserted through observables that cannot move (findings 4, 5);
- no unit test drives `ResilientMessageSender` with a claim that is already taken, no test parks a worker in an interruptible wait across `close()`, and `MockProducer.close(Duration)` hides whether the 10 s budget reaches the producer (findings 22, 24, 26);
- the seed-corpus directories the fuzz workflow and CONTRIBUTING describe do not exist (finding 20).
