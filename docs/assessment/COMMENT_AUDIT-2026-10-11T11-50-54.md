## 1. Executive summary

The comment base of tabellarium remains a knowledge carrier first and a cost factor second, and both earlier remediations hold: for the third audit running the mechanically detectable debt classes are at zero (no commented-out code, no `TODO`, no ceremonial `@param`, no `@param` without a parameter, no unresolved KDoc link - the last guaranteed by Dokka's `failOnWarning`), every one of the 430 tests opens with the three-question block, the ADR-0001 vocabulary is in use at 58 statements (up from 27), and three more hand-maintained mirrors have become tool-checked since September (the metric inventory in three documents, the tag values, the cardinality table, the breaker table). What this round found sits, again, where tooling cannot reach - but the shape has shifted. In September the debt was *a resolving link next to a false sentence*; this time it is **stale pointers and stale rationale under green tooling**: a comment in `append()` that has contradicted the `catch` block sixty lines below it since the day both were written, a rationale that still describes the `contains`-based thread-name matching replaced the same afternoon it was written, a helper KDoc that names a caller the September extraction of `KafkaTransport` removed, and a public KDoc that sends API consumers to an internal class for an inventory that moved to the metrics guide a month ago. All four compile, resolve and read well; none is true. The Mediums are structural: the delivery-ownership protocol - whose hub `DeliveryOwnership` the September remediation created - is still restated at six touchpoints with roughly eighty lines outside the hub (the successor of the "I/O thread" pattern the first two audits tracked), six literal numbers in prose mirror constants that the guide's checked table already carries, the `appender`-tag derivation is duplicated in code with a comment asking the reader to keep the copies in step, and the one public property whose misuse has a security consequence (`debug`) tells operators to "consider removing" it without saying why. The Lows are same-file repetition, caller enumerations in callee KDoc, two chronicles, a prefix used for ordering statements that are not invariants, one regressed date-only report reference and one stale justification in a test block. Comment density is 1.31 in production and 0.80 in tests; the test ratio rose from 0.56 because the appender test was split into eight classes with their own fixture KDoc - the product of the convention, not a symptom, and no quota is proposed.

**Conclusion.**

1. **State:** knowledge, not dead weight. The center of gravity of the debt is *staleness under green tooling* - sentences that resolve or compile but describe a scheme, a caller or a target that moved - followed by *concept scattering* (the ownership protocol, the `max.block.ms` cap rationale) and *numeric mirrors*. Roughly 6-7 % of the production comment lines (about 230 of 3 585) can silently become wrong: the ownership spokes (~80 lines), the duplicated hook and teardown statements (~40), the caller enumerations and entry-point pointers (~30), the numeric mirrors (~25), and the chronicle and derivation lines (~50). Everything symbolic in production is safeguarded; the 120 test-KDoc links are not, and remain a recorded blind spot.
2. **Reducibility and limiting factor:** condensation potential is ~4 % of production comment lines (about 140: ownership spokes ~50, metrics-hook duplication ~25, same-file repetition in the metrics classes ~20, chronicles ~15, caller enumerations ~10, the `internal` paragraph 5, plus ~15 lines in tests). Decomposition potential is zero this round: the locality rule (concept paragraph inside a function body) yields no case in the production tree, as in both earlier audits - every inline block justifies the statement below it. The limiting factors are (a) two carriers that do not exist yet - a single tag-derivation function (finding 5) and a written rule for numbers in prose (pattern P2) - without which the mirrors stay, and (b) the remaining stock being why-knowledge with named failure modes, correctly placed; a reduction beyond ~4 % would remove rationale, not redundancy.
3. **Most essential risks & anomalies:**
   - 🔴 `./src/main/kotlin/eu/inqudium/tabellarium/KafkaAppender.kt:488-491` says a routing failure is reported "without a class tag"; the `catch` sixty lines below and the `KafkaAppenderMetrics` contract report it under `TECHNICAL`. Original to commit `5f2bd57` (2026-08-28), it survived three audits because both halves live in one function.
   - 🟠 Three "green but stale" pointers: the `clientIds` rationale from the `contains` era (`ProducerRegistry.kt:45-46`), the `closeInParallel` user list that predates `KafkaTransport` (`ParallelClose.kt:4-7`), and the public `bindMeterRegistry` KDoc pointing consumers at an internal class for an inventory that is no longer there (`KafkaAppenderMeterRegistry.kt:12-16`).
   - 📚🔴 stock that must not be touched in any hygiene pass: the `MetricsBindings` own-binder rationale (`:23-50`), the `MessageEnricher` key-bounding why-not (`:171-200`), the non-volatile `errorReported` safety argument and the hand-off-first invariant in `ResilientMessageSender` (`:321-331`, `:263-276`), the `BoundedWorkerDispatcher` protocol (`:28-75`, the hub), the `DeliveryOwnership` residual-race paragraph (`:36-44`), the `HalfOpenThrottle` negative-clock why-not (`:79-90`), the `SelfLoggingGuard` three-echoes section (`:17-54`), the `stop()` unbind-last rationale in `KafkaAppender` (`:629-641`), the `LogstashHttpMethodKeyValueIngestTest` incident chronicle - each the only copy of a failure mode.
   - Systemic: the concept-at-every-touchpoint pattern persists with a new subject. The first two audits reduced the "never block the Kafka I/O thread" rationale from six touchpoints to three; the ownership protocol now has a hub and six spokes that have not shrunk since the hub was written.
